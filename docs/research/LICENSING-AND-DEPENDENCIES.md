# Track licenses before packaging dependencies

This inventory is a preliminary engineering screen as of **2026-10-23**, not legal advice. Source code, libraries, symbols, footprints, 3D models and imported user assets can carry different terms even when a parent application has one headline license. Before redistributing binaries or community assets, generate a file-level SBOM/notices, preserve upstream license texts and attribution, and obtain legal review of the exact packaging and linking model.

The desktop preview declares exact Electron and TypeScript versions and commits npm's lockfile. The app's hand-written MCP wire handling remains a feasibility stub and does not implement the current 2026-07-28 protocol revision. Before adding an SDK, verify its exact package version, license, protocol coverage and transport behavior. The Electron bundle also includes third-party Chromium, V8 and Node notices; preserve the upstream `LICENSE` files from the downloaded distribution and conduct a release-specific notice audit before redistribution.

EasyEDA Standard source recovery is feasible from a user-exported local JSON/ZIP: the format documents schematic, symbol, PCB and footprint document types. Build a versioned importer with loss reporting, original-source retention and pin/geometry checks. Do not scrape cloud endpoints or republish vendor/community components whose license is unknown. ([format repository](https://github.com/easyeda/easyeda-std-file-format), [format manual](https://docs.easyeda.com/en/DocumentFormat/EasyEDA-Document-Format/index.html))

## Conclusion

Treat licensing as per-file provenance, not a one-time engine checkbox. A separately installed KiCad process is the cleanest first integration boundary, but it does not itself settle redistribution or linking questions. The owner should approve whether the product will require a user-installed engine or eventually ship a reviewed bundle.
